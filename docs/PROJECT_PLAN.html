SocialMedia_Manager: project plan

Our own app to plan, approve, publish and measure posts on Instagram, Facebook and LinkedIn, covering the parts of Metricool we use. It runs on your laptop first, keeps the code backed up on GitHub, and can move to Hostinger with a login later. It is built with Laravel, using the same working routine as moggies.in.

What you decided (14 Nov 2026)
  • Why our own app: cost, features and data security.
  • Local first: it runs and is managed from your laptop, and GitHub keeps the code as a backup. Hosting on Hostinger with a login comes later.
  • Build with Laravel (PHP).
  • No login for now.
  • More accounts beyond Moggies may be added later.
  • AI caption help and a Claude connector: keep the option open.
  • Accounts: the three connected in Metricool: Facebook Page, Instagram moggies_international, LinkedIn company page.
  • Tasks you asked for: LinkedIn approving company-page access (#10) and audience breakdowns by country, city, age and gender (#30).

2Why, and what success looks like

  • Cost: no monthly subscription. All tools are free; only the optional AI helper costs a little per use.
  • Features: the parts of Metricool we use, plus what it doesn't give us: approval with your brand rules, history that never expires, and a link to Claude later.
  • Data security: your posts, numbers and account access stay on your laptop and in your own encrypted backups, never in GitHub or a third-party tool.

Success measures my proposal

  • No post goes live without your approval.
  • Two weeks of scheduled posts with none missed while the laptop was on, and none more than 5 minutes late.
  • Planning a week of posts for three networks takes under 30 minutes.
  • Our numbers match Meta Business Suite and LinkedIn's page analytics within ±5% for the same period.
  • History kept from 3 Oct 2026 onwards, starting with the saved Metricool export.
  • Only official network APIs are used.

5How we build it

Chosen: Laravel + Filament, local first Your pick

  • A complete app framework: timed jobs, a job queue, encryption, database changes, and a network client with test fakes.
  • Filament gives ready-made screens (forms, tables, dashboard, charts), "brands" for more accounts later, and a two-step login for when the app moves online.
  • Moves later to your existing Hostinger plan (PHP + MySQL + cron) at no extra hosting cost.

Considered and not chosen

  • WordPress on Local: Local would have to be opened and the site started by hand for posts to go out, and it's heavier on memory.
  • Python app like Document Intelligence: nothing to install, but going online would need a paid Hostinger VPS.
  • Next.js: new hosting with monthly bills and large installs.
  • Ready-made tools (Postiz, Mixpost): need a Docker server or a paid edition, and fit your approval rules less well.

How one post travels

  1. You (or Claude, later) write the post once, pick the networks and add photos or video. The app checks each network's rules and shows a preview per network.
  2. The post waits for approval. You tick the brand-rules checklist (no real people's faces, AI people labelled, only approved figures and wording) and approve.
  3. Facebook: the post is handed to Facebook straight away with its time, so it goes out even if the laptop is off.
  4. Instagram: at the set time the app copies the photo to the hidden relay folder, Instagram downloads it, the app publishes, and the copy is deleted.
  5. LinkedIn: at the set time the app uploads the media and posts.
  6. The network returns the post's link and the post shows Published. If something fails, the app retries 3 times over about 15 minutes, then marks it Failed and shows a Windows pop-up with the reason.
  7. Every day the collector saves followers, reach, engagement and audience numbers.

When the laptop is off or asleep

  • Facebook posts still go out (Facebook holds them).
  • Instagram and LinkedIn posts wait. When the app next starts, it lists them as Missed and asks you: post now, pick a new time, or skip. It never posts late on its own.
  • The analytics collector catches up for the days it missed, as far back as each network allows.
  • Tip: leave the laptop on and plugged in at post times. Windows can wake it from sleep for a scheduled job, but not from shut-down.

11Security and privacy

  • Only on this laptop: the app answers only on 127.0.0.1, so other devices on your Wi-Fi can't open it. Your Windows password or PIN protects it. Turn on Windows device encryption if the laptop offers it.
  • Account access (network tokens) is encrypted with the app key. The key lives in the Data folder and in the encrypted backups, never in GitHub.
  • GitHub holds code only. The Data folder sits outside the code folders, and a check in the routine stops data files from being committed.
  • Backups are password-protected zips (AES). You keep the password. Without it a backup can't be opened, by anyone.
  • Photo relay: random file names, deleted right after Instagram downloads them. The folder can't be listed or indexed, and its FTP account can only reach that folder.
  • Only the minimum permissions are asked from Meta and LinkedIn, and the app reminds you before access expires.
  • A short privacy policy for the app goes on moggiesinternational.com (#8), which Meta and LinkedIn ask for.

12Working routine

  1. Claude works in Dev on a new branch, with test data and posting set to dry run.
  2. A code commit, then a docs: commit with the log entry (next SM-0xx id), the tracker and the How-to-use guide.
  3. Claude pushes and gives you the link to open the pull request. You merge it.
  4. You double-click Update and start (it asks before changing anything, like Document Intelligence's). App takes the new version, updates its database and restarts. Claude checks it and marks the item live.
  5. Local (Dev) and live (App) are tracked separately for every item.
  6. You approve each commit. Nothing posts to a real account without your OK.
  7. Each task starts in a fresh session from the tracker and the log, and Claude closes its test browsers after every check.
